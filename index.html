A free Theory of Computation toolkit

DeltaDraw helps students learn automata theory by doing it. Draw a deterministic or nondeterministic finite automaton, run strings through it one symbol at a time, convert an NFA to a DFA, minimize it, and export a clean diagram for your homework as PNG, SVG or LaTeX (TikZ). It runs in your browser with nothing to install and no account.

Available now

  • DFA, NFA and ε-NFA diagrams with curved arrows and self-loops
  • Step-by-step string simulation with highlighted states
  • Step-by-step NFA to DFA conversion and DFA minimization
  • Union, intersection, complement and equivalence of automata
  • Regular expression to NFA and NFA to regular expression, step by step
  • Automatic transition table
  • Export to PNG, SVG, LaTeX/TikZ and JFLAP
  • Chapter 1 practice exercises with exact checking
  • Exam mode for proctored online exams
Is DeltaDraw free?
Yes. It is free and open source, and your drawings stay in your own browser.
Which textbook does it follow?
The chapters follow Sipser's Introduction to the Theory of Computation, which many Theory of Computation courses use.
Can I use the diagrams in a LaTeX report?
Yes. Export LaTeX to get TikZ code, or export an SVG or high-resolution PNG.
