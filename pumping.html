The pumping lemma as a game

A pumping lemma proof is a game against an adversary. The adversary picks the pumping length p, you pick a string s in the language, the adversary splits s any way the lemma allows, and you pick how many times to pump. If you can always pump s out of the language, the language is not regular (Sipser, Theorem 1.70) or not context-free (Theorem 2.34).

A plain string such as 0011 works too, but a proof needs s to grow with p.

How the page checks your proof

  1. It lists every split the lemma allows for the current p and groups them into cases, such as “y has only 0s”.
  2. For each case, it pumps every split with your i and checks whether the result leaves L.
  3. If one split can't be pumped out of L by any i, the adversary wins with that split, and your s cannot work.
  4. When every case is covered, check every p from 3 to 10 and read the proof outline.

Good to know

  • i = 0 (“pumping down”) is allowed and is sometimes the only choice that works, as in Sipser's Example 1.77.
  • Some languages here are regular or context-free. For those the adversary always wins: the lemma really holds for them.
  • Passing the pumping test does not prove that a language is regular or context-free.
